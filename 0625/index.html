IGCSE Physics 0625, paper by paper

Which three papers you actually sit, what each one is worth, and a free trainer for the parts that are technique rather than knowledge.

Every Cambridge IGCSE Physics candidate takes three papers, but not the same three. Which multiple-choice and theory papers you sit depends on whether you have been entered for Core or Extended, and that single decision sets the highest grade you can be awarded. Everyone then takes one practical paper, either Paper 5 or Paper 6.

Below, each paper links to the free CIE Insider trainers built for it.

Core or Extended: which three papers you sit

The decision that sets your grade range
THE TWO TIERS
  • CORE  Paper 1 and Paper 3, questions on the Core subject content only. Eligible for grades C to G.
  • EXTENDED  Paper 2 and Paper 4, questions on the Core and Supplement content. Eligible for grades A* to G.
  • Then one practical paper — Paper 5 or Paper 6 — whichever your centre enters you for.

If you are aiming above a C, you need to be sitting the Extended papers, and a good deal of the Supplement content — momentum, for one — simply does not appear on the Core papers. Check which tier you have been entered for before you plan any revision around past papers.

Frequently asked questions

0625, quickly answered
What papers do you sit for Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625?

All candidates take three papers. Core candidates take Paper 1 and Paper 3; Extended candidates take Paper 2 and Paper 4. Every candidate then takes one practical paper, either Paper 5 or Paper 6.

What is the difference between Core and Extended in IGCSE Physics?

Core questions are based on the Core subject content only, and Core candidates are eligible for grades C to G. Extended questions are based on the Core and Supplement content, and Extended candidates are eligible for grades A* to G.

How long is each 0625 paper and how many marks is it worth?

Paper 1 and Paper 2 are both 45 minutes, 40 marks and 30% of the qualification. Paper 3 and Paper 4 are both 1 hour 15 minutes, 80 marks and 50%. Paper 5 is 1 hour 15 minutes and Paper 6 is 1 hour; both are 40 marks and 20%.

Should I take Paper 5 or Paper 6 in IGCSE Physics?

You take one of the two, and which one is an entry decision made by your centre rather than a choice you make in the exam hall. Paper 5 is the practical test and runs 1 hour 15 minutes; Paper 6 is the alternative to practical and runs 1 hour. Both are worth 40 marks and 20% of the qualification, and both are based on the experimental skills in Section 4 of the syllabus.

Is Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0972 the same as 0625?

Yes, in every way that affects your revision. The syllabus content is identical apart from one sentence about grade letters, and the question papers, mark schemes and examiner reports are the same documents issued under two codes. The difference is the grade scale: 0972 reports 9 to 1, 0625 reports A* to G. There is a longer answer on the blog.
