What you send

Ideally a full past paper you have attempted, with your working as you actually wrote it. If you have not covered enough of the syllabus yet, one question is fine. Not a blank paper, and not a tidied-up version. Most of the marks are in the working, so the messy original is the one I need.

What comes back

Written · within a week, usually sooner

What it is not

Not a tutorial, not a worked solution, and not a re-teach of the topic. You can get the physics explained in a dozen places for free. This tells you what your answer was worth to the person marking it.

No lesson · your paper, not a model answer

Why it has to be your own attempt

A model answer marked out of 6 tells you nothing, because it gets 6. The reason marking is worth anything is that it finds the gap between what you meant and what you wrote down — and that gap is invisible to you, which is exactly why it keeps costing marks.

Why five, why free, and the one condition

There are no places to claim and no form to fill in. The first five scripts I receive get marked. Marking properly, mark by mark against a real scheme, takes about an hour a script, so five is five hours, and five hours is what I can give this week. I would rather promise five and do them properly than promise fifty and do them badly.

Free, because everything on this site so far describes what examiner marking is like, and describing it does not work. Doing it once, on your paper, does. If it turns out to be worth having, I will work out how to offer it properly rather than five at a time. If nobody wants it, that is worth knowing too, and it cost me an evening to find out.

When the five are gone I will say so on this page rather than quietly keep taking them.

The one condition

If you send me work for free marking, you are agreeing that I can use it anonymised in a paid product later — retyped, so no handwriting, no name, no candidate number, no school. I will still come back and check with you before I use your script specifically. If you would rather I did not use your work at all, say so when you send it and I will mark it anyway. That is the exchange: you get an hour of examiner marking, and I get to show other students where real answers lose marks.

Questions

Is this really free?

Yes. No card, no trial that starts billing, no account to make. You email me an answer and I email you back a marked one.

Does it have to be a whole paper?

A whole paper is better, because you usually lose marks the same way more than once, and one question is not enough to show that. But a single question is genuinely useful too, and in September plenty of you will not have covered enough of the syllabus for a full paper. Send what you have.

What if my answer is bad?

Then it is a more useful script to mark than a good one. I have marked thousands of these, plenty of them weak, and it is just the work. The only script I cannot do anything with is a blank one.

What if the five have already gone?

Send it anyway. I will tell you straight away rather than leaving you waiting, and if enough people ask I will work out how to offer it properly. I will not pretend a place is free when it is not.

0625 or 9702?

Either. I marked both for Cambridge and have read every examiner report for both from 2021 to 2026. Say which one you are doing when you send it.

Will you use my work for anything?

Yes, possibly, and that is the condition of the free marking — it is set out above. It would be anonymised: retyped, so no handwriting, no name, no candidate number and no school. I will also come back and check with you before I use your script specifically. If you would rather I did not use your work at all, say so when you send it and I will still mark it; I would just rather you told me than assumed.

What happens to my email address?

It goes on my list, which is where I send physics emails roughly weekly, and there is an unsubscribe link on every one of them. I do not sell it and nobody else sees it.
