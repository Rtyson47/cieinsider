The mark you'd give yourself isn't the mark you'd get

When you mark your own paper, you know what you meant. So when the scheme says "single frequency" and you wrote "single colour", it's easy to tick it, because that's basically what you said. It scores zero. On IGCSE Extended papers, Cambridge's examiner reports flagged "light of a single colour" as not credited in November 2021, November 2022 and June 2025.

At A Level, defining potential difference as energy transferred "by" a unit charge, instead of "per" unit charge, lost the mark on 9702 Paper 22 in November 2022. One word.

Marking strictly is a skill, and it takes practice and someone checking you. Before the exam, what you need is the mark you'd get on a paper you sat today, and where the rest went, so the weeks you have left go on the marks you're losing.

What a note looks like · IGCSE Paper 4 · 6(a)(ii) · 1 mark

You wrote that the current "remains the same". It doesn't. In the dark the LDR's resistance goes up, so the total resistance goes up and the current falls. The voltmeter reading rises because the LDR now takes a bigger share of the 5.0 V. That share is the mark.
