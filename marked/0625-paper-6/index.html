What I mark on Paper 6

Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical) is 1 hour and 40 marks, every question compulsory. Core and Extended candidates sit the same paper, unless their school enters them for Paper 5, the lab practical. It tests experimental skills without a lab: the readings come from diagrams of apparatus on the paper.

SkillWhat the syllabus says it testsWhat I check on your script
Measuring and recordingMaking and recording observations, measurements and estimates.Every reading against the diagram, to the precision the question asks for, and every unit and significant figure in your table.
The graph and the dataInterpreting and evaluating experimental observations and data.Your scale, each plotted point against the grid, the line of best fit, the gradient triangle, and the values you read off it.
Planning and evaluatingPlanning experiments and investigations, and evaluating methods and suggesting improvements.Your plan's variables, apparatus, method, table and conclusion, and whether your fair-test and technique answers are about this experiment.

Three of the notes, next to what the examiner report said about this paper

Note 1 · 1(g), levelling the water · 1 mark lost

"The question asks how to make sure the water levels in beakers X and Y are the same, so the answer has to be about both beakers. Either of these scores:
Lower my head until my eye is level with the water surfaces, and look at both surfaces at the same time.
or: measure the height of the water in each beaker with a ruler."

Examiner report · June 2026 · 0625/62

"Vague references to avoiding parallax errors, without explaining that both surfaces should be viewed simultaneously at eye level, were insufficient."

Note 2 · 2(d)(ii), the fair test · 1 mark lost

"The question asks why your conclusion would still hold if the test were fair. Use the unfairness you found in (i):
The black card was further from the lamp (2.0 cm against 1.0 cm), so it received less radiation, but its temperature still rose faster (3.4 against 1.9 °C/min). At the same distance the difference would be even bigger."

Examiner report · June 2026 · 0625/62

"Most candidates compared the absorbing properties of the black and white cards without referring to the fact that the distances of the cards from the lamp were unequal."

Card D · 3(d), the gradient · scored, made safer

"Good, big triangle, clearly marked. Write the two corners as coordinates, and give G to 2 significant figures. "0.02" looks like 1 s.f. even though your working gives exactly 0.0200:
Corners on the line: (0, 0.34) and (33, 1.00)
G = (1.00 - 0.34) / (33 - 0)
G = 0.66 / 33 = 0.020"

Examiner report · June 2026 · 0625/62

"Some candidates ignored the instructions to indicate on the graph the values chosen to calculate the gradient and to show all working."

Questions about Paper 6 marking

Which Paper 6s can you mark?

Any 0625 Paper 6 from March 2021 to June 2026, every variant. That's 39 papers, and I hold the question paper and the published mark scheme for each one, so I mark against the real scheme. If you've sat a school mock, send it and I'll tell you first whether I can mark it against a real scheme or only give you an opinion.

Paper 5 or Paper 6?

Your school enters you for one of them. Paper 5 is the practical test, and you do the experiments in a lab. Paper 6, the Alternative to Practical, has no experiments: the readings come from diagrams on the paper. I mark both, at £25 each. If you're not sure which you sit, email me first.

How should I print and photograph a practice paper?

Print it at "Actual size" or 100%, not "Fit to page", because the readings are taken with a ruler on the paper. The sample's copy came out at about 97% of full size, which is why its ruler readings were short. Then photograph each page flat and square, with the whole graph grid in the shot.
