Paper 6 marks go on small things

Most of Paper 6 isn't hard physics. The marks go on reading an instruction exactly, giving a value to the right precision, and describing a technique for the apparatus in front of you. These are from Cambridge's examiner report on the June 2026 paper, variant 62:

Examiner report · June 2026 · 0625/62

"Vague references to avoiding parallax errors, without explaining that both surfaces should be viewed simultaneously at eye level, were insufficient."

"Many candidates calculated the difference between the two density values and were unable to proceed further."

"Many candidates started the stopwatch when the ball was released rather than when it passed the first line marked on the cylinder."

Each of these is easy to miss when you mark your own paper, because you know what you meant. That's what the marking is for: the mark you'd get on the day, and a note on exactly where each lost mark went.
