What I mark on Paper 4

Paper 4 (Theory, Extended) is 1 hour 15 minutes and 80 marks, every question compulsory, and it's half of the Extended qualification. It's set on the Core and Supplement content across all six topics, from motion, forces and energy to space physics. Extended candidates sit it with Paper 2. Core candidates sit Paper 3 instead.

Three of the notes, next to what the examiner report said about this paper

Note 2 · 1(b), a falling ball with air resistance · 1 mark lost

"You said air resistance "eventually" acts, then jumped to it balancing the weight. The mark is for the middle: as the ball speeds up, air resistance increases, so the resultant force and the acceleration both decrease."

Examiner report · November 2025 · 0625/43

"Stronger candidates were able to clearly explain the change in motion of the ball as the speed increased causing the air resistance to increase."

Note 4 · 5(c)(ii), the P-wave at the outer core · 1 mark lost

"You got the mark for the speed decreasing, but with the benefit of the doubt (BOD on your script), because the reason you gave is the wrong way round: the outer core is denser than the mantle, not less dense. What slows the P-wave at X is that it passes from solid rock into a liquid, and waves like this travel more slowly in liquids than in solids. The other mark is for naming the process: refraction."

Examiner report · November 2025 · 0625/43

"Stronger candidates gave the process as refraction."

Note 5 · 5(d), greenhouse gases · 2 marks lost

"Two separate points, and both are about what changes. First, the scheme underlines the word increase: greenhouse gases being there doesn't explain a temperature rise, more of them does. Second, the comparison that matters is not what the Sun gives the ground against what the ground gives off. It's how much radiation gets out through the atmosphere."

Examiner report · November 2025 · 0625/43

"Stronger candidates gave a clear explanation linking an increase in greenhouse gases and the amount of radiation that is prevented from escaping the atmosphere."
