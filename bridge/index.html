What changes at A level

The physics gets taught. The maths gets assumed.

You get lessons on the physics. Nobody teaches the maths underneath it. Cambridge lists these six skills in topic 1 of the 9702 syllabus, then expects them in every calculation for two years. None is hard on its own, which is why they go unchecked all summer.

Units and quantities

Working out a quantity's units from scratch, and checking an equation is even possible before you trust it. The IGCSE syllabus never asks for either.

Module 1
Standard form

Multiplying and dividing powers of ten without dropping one. At A level the quantities run from 10-19 up to 108.

Module 2
Unit prefixes

Nano, micro, milli, kilo, mega. Two of them in the same calculation is normal at A level, and that last step is where the marks go.

Module 3
Rearranging first

Getting the formula right with letters before any numbers go in. It is the habit that keeps long A level formulae manageable.

Module 6
Unit conversion

2.0 mm2 is 2.0 x 10-6 m2, not 2.0 x 10-3. Square the factor, not just the unit.

Modules 3 and 4
Significant figures

Matching your data instead of copying out the calculator display. The cheapest mark in physics to lose, and it gets lost all year.

Module 7
Examiner tip

At IGCSE the numbers are kind to you: put them in first and you can still finish the arithmetic, so over two years that becomes the habit. At A level the formulae are longer and the quantity you want is rarely sitting on its own, so numbers first leaves you untangling digits and powers of ten. Letters first, once, is not tidiness. It is what the rest of your working rests on.
