What actually changes from IGCSE

The physics gets harder. The handling gets assumed.

The students I see struggling in the first term are rarely the ones who cannot do the physics. AS Physics is a real step up and nobody is going to pretend otherwise, but the physics is the part you will be taught, and your teacher will take you through it. What no one stops to teach is the numerical machinery underneath it, which the 9702 syllabus lists in topic 1 and then expects for the rest of the course. Here are the six, and where the course deals with each one.

Units and quantities

Reading a quantity straight off its unit, deriving base units for a derived quantity, and checking whether an equation is even homogeneous. The IGCSE syllabus never mentions base units or homogeneity. The AS syllabus makes them outcomes 1.2.2 and 1.2.3. This is also where the notation shifts: m s-1 rather than m/s, negative indices for "per", and the Greek letters you are about to meet everywhere.

Module 1
Standard form

Reading it, writing it, and multiplying and dividing in it without losing a power of ten. The first part has to sit between 1 and 10, so 48 x 10-6 is the right value in the wrong form. At AS the quantities routinely run from 10-19 to 108.

Module 2
Unit prefixes

Swapping nano, micro, milli, kilo and mega for their powers of ten before you calculate rather than after. Two prefixes in one calculation is the normal case at AS, and the marks tend to go in the last step.

Module 3
Rearranging before substituting

Getting the subject right with symbols only, then putting numbers in once. Including the step people drop: rooting the whole right hand side, not part of it.

Module 6
Unit conversion

2.0 mm2 is 2.0 x 10-6 m2, not 2.0 x 10-3. Square the factor, not just the unit, and this one shows up the moment you meet the Young modulus. Plus the everyday conversions AS leans on, like grams to kilograms and cm3 to m3.

Modules 3 and 4
Significant figures

Matching the least precise data instead of copying the calculator display. It is the cheapest mark in physics to lose and it is lost all year.

Module 7
Examiner tip

At IGCSE the numbers are usually kind to you. Questions tend to be structured so that you can put the numbers in first and still finish the arithmetic, and rearranging is easier once they are in. That works for two years, so it becomes the habit. At AS the formulae are longer and the quantity you want is rarely sitting on its own, so substituting first leaves you rearranging an expression cluttered with numbers and powers of ten. Doing it with symbols first, once, is not tidiness at A level. It is the skill the rest of your working rests on.

What is inside

Eight modules, each ending in a drill.

Short and practical. Every module ends with problems set in 9702 contexts, so you are practising the skill where you will actually use it rather than in the abstract. All eight are open from the first day, so take them two a week or all in a weekend. They build in order, each one using only what came before, and the last one puts the whole lot together.

Module 1

Quantities, units and A level notation

Base and derived units, the quantity and symbol table for AS, homogeneity checks, and the notation shift from IGCSE. First, because everything after it is written this way.

Module 2

Standard form

Reading it, writing it, and doing arithmetic in it without losing a power of ten. Set at AS scales.

Module 3

Prefixes and unit magnitudes

The full prefix table, and the area and volume trap where the factor gets squared or cubed.

Module 4

Converting to SI units

The everyday conversions AS leans on, plus the sanity check that catches a wrong answer before you write it down.

Module 5

Using your calculator

Order of operations, entering standard form properly, and the classic error of typing 10 x 10 to the 8 when you meant 1 x 10 to the 8.

Module 6

Rearranging equations

Symbols first, on real 9702 equations: v = f lambda, rho = RA/L, E = half m v squared, p = mv, V = IR, P = VI.

Module 7

Significant figures

Counting them, rounding to match your data, and why quoting the full display costs marks in every paper you will sit.

Module 8

How to attack any calculation

The capstone. A seven step method that works on every numerical question, walked through a suvat problem and a resistivity problem end to end, using all six skills above.
