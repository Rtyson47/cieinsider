About this tool

AS Physics definitions and formulae for 9702 Paper 2

Definitions and laws are tested on almost every AS paper. On Paper 2 you write them out, for example “Define density” (June 2021, Paper 21). On Paper 1 you choose between near-identical wordings: “What is the definition of density?” (June 2025, Paper 13) offered “mass of one cubic metre”, “mass of a unit volume”, “mass per cubic metre” and “mass per unit volume”. Only the last is the definition.

One word changes the mark. The November 2021 Paper 23 report says velocity as “the rate of change of displacement” is correct, but “rate of change of distance” or “speed in a particular direction” is not.

Formulae printed on the exam data sheet, such as the Doppler equation and I = Anvq, are marked “on data sheet”. Choose Formulae to learn to drill only the ones you have to memorise.

Measurement · definitions

Define: Scalar
Quantity with magnitude only
Define: Vector
Quantity with both magnitude and direction
State: SI base quantities and their units
mass (kg), length (m), time (s), current (A), temperature (K), amount of substance (mol)
State: SI prefixes from pico to tera
pico p 10⁻¹², nano n 10⁻⁹, micro μ 10⁻⁶, milli m 10⁻³, centi c 10⁻², deci d 10⁻¹, kilo k 10³, mega M 10⁶, giga G 10⁹, tera T 10¹²
Define: Random error
Unpredictable variation about a mean; reduced by repeating and averaging
Define: Systematic error
Consistent error in one direction; cannot be reduced by repeating measurements
Define: Zero error
Systematic error where an instrument gives a non-zero reading when the true value is zero
Define: Precision
Results clustered closely together / small range of values
Define: Accuracy
Result close to the true / accepted value
Define: Uncertainty
Range of values within which the true value is expected to lie

Kinematics · definitions

Define: Displacement
Distance moved in a specified direction
Define: Distance
Total length of the path travelled (a scalar)
Define: Speed
Distance travelled per unit time
Define: Velocity
Rate of change of displacement
Define: Acceleration
Rate of change of velocity
Define: Free fall
Motion under gravity alone (no air resistance / other forces)

Dynamics · definitions

Define: Linear momentum
Product of mass and velocity
Define: Force
Rate of change of momentum
State what is meant by: Mass
The property of an object that resists change in its motion
State: Newton’s first law
A body remains at rest or continues with constant velocity unless acted on by a resultant force
State: Newton’s second law
The resultant force on a body is proportional to its rate of change of momentum (and acts in the same direction)
State: Newton’s third law
When body A exerts a force on body B, body B exerts an equal and opposite force on body A (of the same type)
Define: Impulse
Product of force and the time for which it acts; equal to change in momentum
State: Principle of conservation of momentum
The total momentum of a system remains constant provided no external resultant force acts on it
Define: Elastic collision
Collision in which total kinetic energy is conserved; relative speed of approach = relative speed of separation
Define: Inelastic collision
Collision in which total kinetic energy is not conserved (momentum is still conserved)
Explain: Terminal velocity
Constant velocity reached when the resistive (drag) force has increased to equal the driving force, so the resultant force is zero

Forces · definitions

Define: Density
Mass per unit volume
Define: Pressure
Force per unit area (acting perpendicularly to the surface)
Define: Moment of a force
Force × perpendicular distance from the pivot (point) to the line of action of the force
Define: Couple
A pair of equal and opposite forces whose lines of action are not the same; produces rotation only
Define: Torque of a couple
Product of one of the forces and the perpendicular distance between the forces
State: Principle of moments
For a body in equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of anticlockwise moments about the same point
Define: Centre of gravity
Point at which the entire weight of the body may be considered to act
State: Conditions for equilibrium (both)
Resultant force is zero AND resultant torque / moment about any point is zero
Explain: Origin of upthrust
Hydrostatic pressure increases with depth, so the pressure on the bottom of the object is greater than on the top; the difference gives an upward force

Work & Energy · definitions

Define: Work done
Product of force and displacement in the direction of the force
Define: Power
Work done per unit time / rate of energy transfer
State: Principle of conservation of energy
Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be converted from one form to another
Define: Efficiency
Ratio of useful energy output to total energy input (expressed as a fraction or percentage)
Define: Gravitational potential energy
Energy stored in a body by virtue of its position in a gravitational field
Define: Kinetic energy
Energy a body has by virtue of its motion

Deformation · definitions

Define: Tensile stress
Force per unit cross-sectional area
Define: Tensile strain
Extension per unit original length
Define: Young modulus
Tensile stress divided by tensile strain (within the limit of proportionality)
State: Hooke’s law
Extension is proportional to the applied force provided the limit of proportionality is not exceeded
Define: Spring constant
Force per unit extension
Define: Elastic deformation
Deformation from which the material returns to its original shape when the load is removed
Define: Plastic deformation
Deformation which is permanent; material does not return to its original shape when the load is removed
Define: Elastic limit
The maximum force / stress beyond which the material does not return to its original length when the load is removed
Define: Limit of proportionality
Point beyond which extension is no longer proportional to the applied force

Waves · definitions

Define: Displacement (waves)
Distance and direction of a particle from its equilibrium position
Define: Progressive wave
A wave that transfers energy (in the direction of propagation)
Define: Amplitude
Maximum displacement from equilibrium
Define: Period
Time for one complete oscillation
Define: Frequency
Number of complete oscillations per unit time
Define: Wavelength
Minimum distance between two points in phase on a wave / distance between adjacent crests
Define: Phase difference
Fraction of a cycle by which one wave leads or lags another; measured in degrees or radians
Define: Wave speed
Distance travelled by a wavefront per unit time
Define: Intensity
Power transmitted per unit area perpendicular to the direction of wave travel
Define: Transverse wave
Oscillations perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer
Define: Longitudinal wave
Oscillations parallel to the direction of energy transfer
Define: Polarisation
Restriction of transverse wave oscillations to one plane
State: Properties of all electromagnetic waves in free space
All are transverse and all travel at the same speed c (3.00 × 10⁸ m/s) in free space
State: Wavelength range of visible light
400–700 nm (in free space)
State: Approximate wavelengths of the EM spectrum
Typical orders of magnitude: radio above about 10⁻¹ m; microwaves 10⁻³ to 10⁻¹ m; infrared 7 × 10⁻⁷ to 10⁻³ m; visible 4 to 7 × 10⁻⁷ m; ultraviolet 10⁻⁸ to 4 × 10⁻⁷ m; X-rays 10⁻¹² to 10⁻⁸ m; γ-rays below about 10⁻¹¹ m (X-ray and γ ranges overlap)

Superposition · definitions

State: Principle of superposition
When two or more waves meet, the resultant displacement is the vector sum of the individual displacements
Define: Coherence
Constant phase difference (and same frequency) between two sources
Define: Interference
Superposition of waves from two coherent sources
Define: Constructive interference
Waves meet in phase, giving maximum amplitude; for sources in phase, path difference = nλ
Define: Destructive interference
Waves meet in antiphase, giving minimum (or zero) amplitude; for sources in phase, path difference = (n + ½)λ
Define: Diffraction
Spreading of a wave as it passes through a gap or around an obstacle
Define: Standing wave
Wave pattern formed by superposition of two waves of the same frequency and amplitude travelling in opposite directions
Define: Node
Point on a stationary wave where the amplitude is zero
Define: Antinode
Point on a stationary wave where the amplitude is maximum

Electricity · definitions

Define: Electric current
Rate of flow of charge
State: Charge is quantised
Charge exists only in whole-number multiples of the elementary charge e (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C)
Define: Potential difference
Work done per unit charge moved between two points
Define: Resistance
Ratio of potential difference across a component to the current through it: R = V/I
Define: The ohm
Resistance of a component with a p.d. of 1 V across it when the current is 1 A (1 Ω = 1 V A⁻¹)
State: Ohm’s law
Current is directly proportional to potential difference provided temperature (and other physical conditions) remain constant
Explain: Why the resistance of a filament lamp increases as the current increases
A larger current heats the filament; its temperature rises, so its resistance increases
Describe: Light-dependent resistor (LDR)
Resistance decreases as light intensity increases
Describe: Thermistor (NTC)
Resistance decreases as temperature increases
Define: Electromotive force (e.m.f.)
Energy transferred per unit charge in driving charge around a complete circuit (from other forms to electrical)
Define: Internal resistance
Resistance to current flow within the source itself

D.C. Circuits · definitions

State: Kirchhoff’s first law
The sum of currents entering a junction equals the sum of currents leaving it (conservation of charge)
State: Kirchhoff’s second law
The sum of the e.m.f.s around a closed loop equals the sum of the p.d.s around the same loop (conservation of energy)
Define: Terminal potential difference
Potential difference across the terminals of a source when current is flowing; equal to e.m.f. minus the lost volts (V = ε − Ir)
Explain: Null method (potentiometer / galvanometer)
Adjust the circuit until the galvanometer reads zero; the two potential differences being compared are then equal

Particle physics · definitions

Define: Nucleon number A (mass number)
Total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus
Define: Proton number Z (atomic number)
Number of protons in the nucleus
Define: Isotopes
Atoms of the same element with the same proton number but different nucleon numbers (different numbers of neutrons)
State: Quantities conserved in nuclear processes
Nucleon number and charge
Define: Unified atomic mass unit (u)
One twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom; 1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg
Describe: Alpha particle (α)
Helium nucleus: 2 protons and 2 neutrons; relative charge +2, relative mass 4
Describe: Beta-minus particle (β⁻)
Fast-moving electron; relative charge −1, negligible mass
Describe: Beta-plus particle (β⁺)
Fast-moving positron; relative charge +1, negligible mass
Describe: Gamma radiation (γ)
High-frequency electromagnetic radiation from nucleus; no charge, no mass
State: The extra particle emitted in β⁻ and in β⁺ decay
β⁻ decay: an (electron) antineutrino. β⁺ decay: an (electron) neutrino
Explain: Why α-particles have discrete energies but β-particles have a continuous range
In β-decay an (anti)neutrino is also emitted and shares the energy released, so β-particle energies vary; α-decay has no third particle
Define: Antiparticle
Particle with the same mass but opposite charge to its corresponding particle
Define: Fundamental particle
A particle that cannot be divided into smaller particles (has no internal structure), e.g. quarks, leptons
Define: Quark (name all 6 flavours)
Fundamental particle; six flavours: up (u), down (d), strange (s), charm (c), top (t), bottom (b)
State: Quark charges (up, down, strange, charm, top, bottom)
up: +⅔e | down: −⅓e | strange: −⅓e | charm: +⅔e | top: +⅔e | bottom: −⅓e
State: Charge of an antiquark
Opposite to the charge of its quark (e.g. anti-up −⅔e, anti-down +⅓e)
State: Proton quark composition
uud (two up quarks, one down quark)
State: Neutron quark composition
udd (one up quark, two down quarks)
State: Quark change in β⁻ decay
A down quark changes to an up quark (neutron udd → proton uud)
State: Quark change in β⁺ decay
An up quark changes to a down quark (proton uud → neutron udd)
Define: Hadron
Particle made of quarks; either a baryon (3 quarks) or a meson (quark + antiquark)
Define: Lepton
Fundamental particle not made of quarks; e.g. electron, neutrino
