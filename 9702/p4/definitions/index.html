About this tool

A Level Physics definitions and formulae for 9702 Paper 4

Every Paper 4 from 2021 to 2026 asks you to define a quantity or state a law, usually several times. Typical questions are “Define gravitational potential at a point” (June 2024, Paper 41) and “State what is meant by simple harmonic motion” (June 2025, Paper 42).

Half a definition gets half the marks. On March 2026 Paper 42, the definition of simple harmonic motion scored one mark for “acceleration is proportional to displacement” and a second for “in the opposite direction”. The examiner’s report says candidates usually knew the first part but often needed to be clearer on the second.

Paper 4 prints the AS data sheet plus a page of A Level formulae, including gravitational and electric potential, capacitor discharge and radioactive decay. Those cards are marked “on data sheet”. Choose Formulae to learn to drill only the ones you have to memorise.

Circular motion · definitions

Define: The radian
Unit of angle; angle subtended at the centre of a circle by an arc equal in length to the radius; 1 rad ≈ 57.3°
Define: Angular velocity ω
Angle swept per unit time; ω = θ/t = 2π/T = 2πf

Gravitational fields · definitions

Define: Gravitational field strength g
Force per unit mass (acting on a small test mass)
State: Newton’s law of gravitation
The gravitational force between two point masses is proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation
Define: Gravitational potential ϕ
Work done per unit mass in bringing a small test mass from infinity to that point
Define: Equipotential surface
Surface on which the gravitational potential is the same at all points
Describe: Geostationary orbit
Equatorial orbit, west to east, period 24 hours, so the satellite stays above the same point on the Earth’s surface

Temperature · definitions

Define: Absolute zero
The lowest possible temperature: zero kelvin on the thermodynamic scale (0 K = −273.15 °C)
Define: Thermal equilibrium
Objects in thermal contact are at the same temperature, so there is no net transfer of thermal energy between them
Define: Specific heat capacity
Energy required per unit mass to raise the temperature by 1 K (with no change of state)
Define: Specific latent heat of fusion
Energy required per unit mass to change state from solid to liquid at constant temperature
Define: Specific latent heat of vaporisation
Energy required per unit mass to change state from liquid to gas at constant temperature

Ideal gases · definitions

State all five assumptions of an ideal gas
1. Molecules have negligible volume compared to container 2. Collisions are perfectly elastic 3. No intermolecular forces except during collisions 4. Time of collision negligible compared to time between collisions 5. Large number of molecules in random motion
Define: Ideal gas
A gas that obeys pV ∝ T, where T is the thermodynamic temperature
Define: Avogadro constant NA
Number of atoms / molecules in one mole of a substance (6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)
Define: Boltzmann constant k
k = R / NA; relates mean kinetic energy of a molecule to temperature

Thermodynamics · definitions

Define: Internal energy
Sum of the random kinetic and potential energies of all the molecules / atoms of the system
State: First law of thermodynamics
Increase in internal energy = heat supplied to system + work done on system (ΔU = q + W)

Oscillations · definitions

Define: Angular frequency ω
2πf = 2π/T; relates frequency of oscillation to angular measure
Define: Simple harmonic motion (SHM)
Acceleration proportional to displacement from a fixed point and directed towards that point
Define: Free oscillation
Oscillation at natural frequency with no energy input / no resistive forces
Define: Resonance
When driving frequency equals natural frequency, resulting in maximum amplitude
Define: Damping
Progressive reduction in amplitude of oscillation due to energy loss to surroundings
Describe: Light damping
Oscillates with decreasing amplitude
Describe: Critical damping
Returns to equilibrium in shortest time without oscillating
Describe: Heavy damping
Returns to equilibrium slowly without oscillating

Electric fields · definitions

Define: Electric field strength E
Force per unit positive charge (acting on a small stationary test charge)
Define: Electric potential V
Work done per unit positive charge in bringing a small test charge from infinity to that point
State: Link between electric field strength and potential
Electric field strength at a point = −(potential gradient) at that point

Capacitance · definitions

Define: Capacitance
Charge stored per unit potential difference (across the capacitor)
Define: Time constant τ
Time for charge / voltage / current to fall to 1/e (≈37%) of its initial value; τ = RC

Magnetic fields · definitions

Define: Magnetic field
Region where a force is exerted on a current-carrying conductor, a moving charge or a magnetic material
Define: Magnetic flux density B
Force per unit current per unit length on a straight conductor placed at right angles to the field; unit tesla (T)
Define: The tesla
The magnetic flux density that gives a force of 1 N per metre on a wire carrying 1 A at right angles to the field (1 T = 1 N A⁻¹ m⁻¹)
State: Fleming’s left-hand rule
First finger: field. Second finger: current. Thumb: force (motion). All three at right angles
Define: Magnetic flux Φ
Product of magnetic flux density and the area perpendicular to the field: Φ = BA
Define: Magnetic flux linkage
Product of magnetic flux and number of turns: NΦ
State: Faraday’s law
The magnitude of the induced e.m.f. is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage
State: Lenz’s law
The induced current / e.m.f. is in a direction that opposes the change causing it

Alternating currents · definitions

Define: Root-mean-square (r.m.s.) current
The steady direct current that would deliver the same power as the alternating current in a resistive load
Define: Peak value (I₀ or V₀)
Maximum value of the alternating current or voltage
State: Mean power in a resistive load (sinusoidal a.c.)
Half the maximum power: ⟨P⟩ = ½I₀V₀
Define: Rectification
Conversion of alternating current (a.c.) to direct current (d.c.)
Describe: Half-wave rectification
A single diode lets current through in only one half of each cycle
Describe: Full-wave rectification
A bridge of four diodes makes the current through the load flow in the same direction in both halves of each cycle

Quantum physics · definitions

Define: Photon
Quantum of electromagnetic energy
Define: Photoelectric effect
Emission of electrons from a (metal) surface when electromagnetic radiation of high enough frequency is incident on it
Define: Work function Φ
Minimum energy required to remove an electron from the surface of a metal
Define: Threshold frequency
Minimum frequency of incident radiation required to emit an electron from a metal surface
Define: Threshold wavelength
Maximum wavelength of incident radiation that can cause photoelectric emission from a metal surface
Define: de Broglie wavelength
Wavelength associated with a moving particle; λ = h/p
Explain: Wave–particle duality
Both radiation and particles show wave and particle behaviour: the photoelectric effect is evidence for the particle nature of EM radiation; interference and diffraction (including electron diffraction) are evidence for wave nature
Define: Electronvolt (eV)
Energy gained by an electron accelerated through a potential difference of 1 V; 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

Nuclear physics · definitions

Define: Radioactive decay
Spontaneous and random disintegration of an unstable nucleus
Explain: Random (radioactive decay)
You cannot predict when a particular nucleus will decay, or which nucleus will decay next
Explain: Spontaneous (radioactive decay)
The decay is not affected by external factors (such as temperature or pressure)
Define: Activity
Number of disintegrations per unit time
Define: Decay constant λ
Probability of decay of a nucleus per unit time
Define: Half-life
Time for the activity (or number of undecayed nuclei) to fall to half its initial value
Define: Mass defect
Difference between the mass of the separated nucleons and the mass of the nucleus
Define: Binding energy
Energy required to separate a nucleus into its constituent nucleons / energy released when nucleus is assembled from its nucleons
Define: Binding energy per nucleon
Total binding energy divided by the number of nucleons in the nucleus
Define: Nuclear fission
Splitting of a heavy nucleus into two smaller nuclei, releasing energy
Define: Nuclear fusion
Joining of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus, releasing energy

Medical physics · definitions

Define: Specific acoustic impedance Z
Z = ρc, where ρ is the density of the medium and c is the speed of sound in the medium
Define: Attenuation coefficient μ
Measure of how rapidly ultrasound or X-ray intensity decreases in a medium; used in I = I₀e⁻μx
Define: Tracer (medical)
Substance containing radioactive nuclei introduced into the body and absorbed by the tissue being studied
Define: Annihilation
When a particle meets its antiparticle; mass–energy and momentum are conserved; produces two gamma photons travelling in opposite directions
Define: Contrast (X-ray image)
Difference in degree of blackening between different tissues in the image

Astronomy · definitions

Define: Luminosity L
Total power of electromagnetic radiation emitted by a star
Define: Radiant flux intensity F
Power of radiation received per unit area perpendicular to direction of travel; F = L / (4πd²)
Define: Standard candle
Object of known luminosity; used to determine distances to galaxies
State: Wien’s displacement law
The wavelength of peak emission is inversely proportional to the surface temperature: λ_max ∝ 1/T
Define: Redshift
Increase in observed wavelength of electromagnetic radiation from a receding source; evidence that the Universe is expanding
State: Hubble’s law
The recession speed of a galaxy is approximately proportional to its distance from us: v ≈ H₀d
