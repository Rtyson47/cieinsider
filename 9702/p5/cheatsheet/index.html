About this sheet

What Paper 5 tests, and how it is marked

Cambridge A Level Physics 9702 Paper 5 (Planning, Analysis and Evaluation) is a 30-mark paper sat in 1 hour 15 minutes. Question 1 (15 marks) asks you to plan an experiment to test a given relationship. It is marked in four fixed groups: defining the problem (2), methods of data collection (4), method of analysis (3) and additional detail including safety (6). Question 2 (15 marks) gives you data to process: a results table with uncertainties, a graph with error bars, a best-fit and worst acceptable line, the gradient (and usually the y-intercept) with uncertainties, then the constants and a final calculated value.

Question 2 comes in two layouts. In most papers part (c)(iv) asks for the y-intercept. In 11 of the 42 papers, including all four June 2026 variants, there is no (c)(iv); a short part (d) asks for a value and its uncertainty from repeated readings or given data, and the paper ends at (f). This sheet covers both.

Drill the graph skills in the linearisation trainer, check a practice answer against the Paper 5 self-assessment checklist, and practise propagation in the uncertainties trainer.

Question 1 · Planning

15 marks The four mark groups are the same in every scheme from March 2021 to June 2026.

Mark groups

Where the 15 marks are

GroupMarksWhat earns them
Defining the problem2Name the independent and dependent variables (or "vary X and measure Y"). Name the quantity that must be kept constant, e.g. "keep E constant".
Methods of data collection4A labelled diagram of a workable set-up. How each variable is measured, naming the instrument.
Method of analysis3The graph to plot, axes named. The validity statement. The constant written as the subject of an expression using the gradient (or intercept).
Additional detail6Up to 6 from the scheme's list of 10 or 11 points (D1 to D10/D11): safety, extra measurements, control methods, precision.

Control variables

Two separate marks

  • Defining the problem: the quantity the experiment needs held fixed, usually one, sometimes two. "Keep E constant" (June 2025 /51); "keep B and t constant" (Nov 2022 /51).
  • Additional detail: every other quantity in the equation that isn't the IV, the DV or a constant like g. Same paper, D2: "keep N and A and l and S constant".
  • A method that keeps one of them constant can earn another D-mark: switch off between readings so the resistor cools (June 2025 /51 D3), tape or plasticine to hold a spacing (June 2022 /52 D8).
"Control the variables" scores nothingThe June 2025 /51 report: credit was not given for "control" because it only repeats the question. Name each quantity.

Validity statement (1 mark)

relationship is valid if a straight line is produced
… passing through the origin
… with y-intercept = ln Z June 2024 /51
… with gradient = −1 (lg–lg version) June 2025 /51

The June 2025 /51 report says you need the words "relationship is valid if" and "straight". Only say "through the origin" when the equation has no intercept term.

Axes and the constant

  • Name both axes in words or on a sketch. Writing y = mx + c under the equation does not count (June 2025 /51 report).
  • Make the constant the subject: "K = expression × gradient", not "gradient = …" (same report).
  • When the power is given, a log graph is sometimes allowed as an equivalent (June 2025 /51) but not always (June 2021 /52: "not logarithmic graphs"). Plot what the equation gives you.

Methods of data collection

The diagram

  • A workable arrangement that could be built, not a list of apparatus.
  • Label the key apparatus by name (e.g. oscilloscope and a.c. supply, June 2025 /51).
  • Voltmeters and oscilloscopes in parallel, ammeters in series. The June 2025 /51 report lists voltmeters or oscilloscopes drawn in series as a common error.
  • Say what kind of supply: "a.c." or a labelled signal generator if frequency is varied.
  • Rulers clamped and aligned with what they measure; Hall probe drawn where B is measured.

Measuring each variable

  • Oscilloscope: V = y-gain × height of the trace; T = time-base × horizontal length of one cycle, f = 1/T (June 2025 /51).
  • Length: ruler or calipers. A micrometer only for small lengths such as a wire diameter (June 2025 /51 report).

Additional detail · 6 marks

Points the schemes list

  • Safety tied to this experiment: hot coil → gloves (June 2025 /51); falling mass → cushion or sand box (June 2023 /51); spring or magnet flying → goggles or safety screen (June 2024 /52); liquid spills → tray (March 2023 /52).
  • Repeat and average the dependent variable at each value.
  • Extra quantities, instrument and formula: diameter by micrometer or calipers, A = πd²/4 (June 2022 /52); k = mg / extension (June 2024 /52); density = mass / volume (Nov 2022 /52).
  • Repeat a measurement in different places and average, e.g. diameter along the coil (June 2025 /51 D5).
  • Make readings larger to cut percentage uncertainty: a larger d to increase the fall time (June 2023 /51).
  • Light gates: one at each end of the distance, connected to a timer (June 2023 /51); v = L/t for a single gate (March 2022 /52); set the height so the ball's diameter cuts the beam (Nov 2025 /51).
  • Temperature: water bath, wait until the temperature is uniform (June 2024 /51); several sensors averaged (June 2024 /51); stir the liquid (Nov 2024 /52).
  • Wait for steady conditions, e.g. a steady air flow before timing (March 2023 /52, Nov 2025 /52).
  • Magnetic field: calibrated Hall probe (Nov 2022 /51), rotated to the maximum reading (Nov 2024 /51), or measured both ways and averaged (March 2025 /52).
  • Oscilloscope settings: adjust y-gain for a large trace; measure n cycles and divide by n (June 2025 /51 D9).
  • Fast events: video and replay (June 2024 /52). Reference marks: fiducial markers (Nov 2021 /52), plumb line (June 2025 /54).
Generic lab rules earn nothing"Check all connections" or "make sure the apparatus works" is normal procedure and is not credited (June 2025 /51 report). Every point must belong to this experiment.

Part (b)

The results table

  • Significant figures: a processed value gets the same number of s.f. as the raw data, or one more. I to 3 s.f. means 1/I to 3 or 4 s.f. (June 2025 /51 report).
  • Logarithms: the number of decimal places matches the raw data's significant figures. Raw data to 3 s.f. means lg values to 3 d.p. (June 2022 /51 report).
  • Round, don't truncate: 2.198 is 2.20, not 2.19 (June 2025 /51 report).
  • Uncertainties for every row, propagated from the raw uncertainty. A single ± value copied down every row is a listed error (same report).

Part (c)

Graph rules from the schemes

  • Points within half a small square; point diameter under half a small square.
  • All six error bars plotted, symmetrical, length within half a small square. A missing bar stops the worst-line mark.
  • Best-fit line with points balanced either side. Never join the top and bottom points.
  • Worst acceptable line: the steepest or shallowest line through every error bar. A dashed line must cross each bar (June 2025 /51 report).
  • Label both lines.
  • Line thickness under half a small square: in every scheme from June 2025.

Parts (c)(iii) and (c)(iv)

Gradient and intercept

  • Read two points on the line, more than half the line apart. Choose points on grid lines; don't use table values (June 2025 /51 report).
  • Show the substitution: (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁) with the numbers written in.
  • Uncertainty in the gradient = best − worst, or ½(steepest − shallowest). Both are accepted.
  • y-intercept: substitute a point and the gradient into y = mx + c. Do the same for the worst line and subtract.
False originIf the x-axis doesn't start at zero, the intercept can't be read off the graph. Schemes state "Do not accept ECF from false origin method" (June 2025 /51).

Parts (d) to (f)

Constants and final answers

  • The constant's value: 2 or 3 s.f. (June 2026 /51, Nov 2022 /52). Its SI unit with the right power of ten is a separate mark.
  • A final calculated value: at least 2 s.f. with the correct power of ten.
  • Write the equation, then the substitution, then the answer and unit. The reports name setting out as what stronger candidates did (June 2025 /51).

Layout B part (d): repeated readings

0.826, 0.829, 0.824, 0.821 m
mean = 0.825 m
uncertainty = ½ × range = ½ × (0.829 − 0.821) = 0.004 m
D = (0.825 ± 0.004) m June 2026 /52

From repeated readings, the absolute uncertainty is half the range (Nov 2022 /51 report).

Uncertainties

Propagation rules

y = 1/xΔy = Δx / x² (fractional: Δy/y = Δx/x)
y = ln xΔy = Δx / x
y = lg xΔy = 0.434 × Δx / x
y = xnΔy/y = |n| × Δx/x (√x: half the fraction)
y = AB or A/BΔy/y = ΔA/A + ΔB/B (add percentages)
y = A ± BΔy = ΔA + ΔB (add absolute values)
(7850 ± 50) − (1250 ± 50) = 6600 ± 100, June 2026 /51

Percentage uncertainty in a constant

Add the percentage uncertainty of every quantity in its expression. Which ones depends on the paper:

gradient + y-intercept Nov 2023 /51
gradient + a given value (ΔE/E) June 2025 /51
gradient + intercept + a given value June 2025 /52
gradient + the (d) value Nov 2022 /52

Max/min working is also accepted: work out the largest and smallest possible values of the constant and use the difference. 28 of the 42 schemes state this.

When the constant is 10c or ec

k = 10^c → Δk = 10^c(best) − 10^c(worst) June 2025 /54
R₀ = e^c → ΔR₀ = e^c(worst) − R₀ Nov 2021 /52

The fractional rules don't apply here. If the constant is the gradient itself (n in a lg–lg graph), its absolute uncertainty is the gradient's (June 2022 /51).

Part (a)

Rearranging into y = mx + c: real examples

E = 3IR + 4IZ
→ 1/I = (3/E)R + 4Z/E
plot 1/I against R · gradient 3/E · intercept 4Z/E March 2024 /52
Z/R = 4ρL / (πYd²)
→ L = (πYZd² / 4ρ) × (1/R)
plot L against 1/R · gradient πYZd²/4ρ · no intercept Nov 2022 /51
pattern: y = A e−kx
→ ln y = −kx + ln A
plot ln y against x · gradient −k · intercept ln A
pattern: y = A xn
→ lg y = n lg x + lg A
plot lg y against lg x · gradient n · intercept lg A

If the rearranged equation has no constant term, part (a) asks for the gradient only. Don't invent an intercept.

Where marks are lost

Each point is named in an examiner report or a mark scheme for the series shown.
  1. Writing "control the variables" instead of naming each quantity (June 2025 /51).
  2. Missing the other equation quantities in the additional-detail list (June 2025 /51 D2).
  3. Meters in series, or no a.c. supply named, on a circuit diagram (June 2025 /51).
  4. Vague safety or generic lab rules not tied to the experiment (June 2025 /51).
  5. Axes not stated, only y = mx + c written (June 2025 /51).
  6. "Through the origin" when the equation has an intercept term.
  7. Logs taken of an equation that is already linear in sin θ or cos θ (March 2022 /52, June 2025 /54).
  8. Too few s.f. in the table, or values truncated (June 2025 /51).
  9. Log values to the wrong number of decimal places (June 2022 /51).
  10. Best-fit line joined top point to bottom point (June 2025 /51 scheme: not accepted).
  11. Worst line missing an error bar, or a dashed line that skips one (June 2025 /51).
  12. Gradient from table values or a triangle under half the line (June 2025 /51).
  13. Intercept read off a false origin (June 2025 /51 scheme: no ECF).
  14. Percentage uncertainty from the gradient alone when the constant also uses the intercept or a given value.
  15. Uncertainty from repeated readings not taken as half the range (Nov 2022 /51).
  16. Wrong unit or power of ten on the constant (Nov 2022 /51).
