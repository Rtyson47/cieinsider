Cambridge Physics Examiner Report 2025: What You Actually Need to Know

What the June 2025 reports for 0625 and 9702 said, quoted with the paper and question, and what to do about each one.

After every exam series, Cambridge publishes a Principal Examiner Report for each paper. It says which questions went badly, what wrong answers candidates gave, and what the mark scheme wanted instead. It is written for teachers.

This post goes through the June 2025 reports for IGCSE Physics (0625) and A Level Physics (9702), paper by paper. Every boxed line is quoted from the report, with the paper and question it comes from, so you can check it yourself. The text around the boxes is mine.

How to use this: read the section for your exam. For each box, ask whether you'd have made that mistake. If you would, that's the thing to practise, not the whole topic.

IGCSE Physics 0625: what the June 2025 reports say

Paper 2 (Extended multiple choice)

The multiple-choice report names the questions where one wrong option was popular. Four of them come from a fact remembered half-right.

0625/21 · Question 16 · thermal radiation

“Although most candidates correctly recalled that black surfaces are better absorbers of radiation than white surfaces, many of these candidates thought that black surfaces are worse emitters of radiation, and therefore chose option C.”

0625/21 · Question 26 · resistance of a wire

“A significant number of candidates did not realise that because R is inversely proportional to the cross-sectional area of the wire, R must be inversely proportional to d², and therefore chose option C instead of option D.”

0625/21 · Question 29 · induced current

“A significant number chose option B, suggesting that they used the left-hand rule instead of the right-hand rule.”

0625/21 · Question 32 · transformer

“A significant number of weaker candidates had the misconception that the iron core was used to conduct current from the primary coil to the secondary coil.”

Black is a better emitter and a better absorber. Area goes with d². The right-hand rule is for generators, the left-hand rule for motors. The iron core links the magnetic field of the two coils; it doesn't carry current between them. If any of those made you pause, the 0625 definitions drill and the MCQ trainer are the quickest fix.

Paper 4 (Extended theory)

0625/41 · Question 1(a) · vectors

“A common error was to identify speed as a vector.”

0625/41 · Question 5(c)(i) · monochromatic light

“Many candidates stated clearly that monochromatic light is light of a single frequency (or wavelength). Weaker candidates often gave the insufficient answer that it is light of a single colour.”

0625/41 · Question 6(a) · magnetic materials

“Many candidates confused magnetic materials with magnets.”

“Single colour” is the everyday version of the definition; the mark is for single frequency or wavelength. The report's key messages for the same paper also say this, which costs marks on any question:

0625/41 · Key messages

“If an answer is changed, it should be crossed out and the new answer written in a blank space. An answer should never overwrite another answer.”

The Core theory report (0625/31) adds one on numbers: “Candidates should be encouraged not to round to 1 significant figure.”

Paper 6 (Alternative to Practical)

0625/61 · Question 4 · planning

“Candidates were expected to explain how to reach a conclusion by drawing a graph of the diameter against resistance, or by comparing values from the table. The question did not ask for a prediction. Some candidates wrote a prediction but gave no explanation of how to reach a conclusion.”

0625/61 · Question 3(e) · equal within experimental accuracy

“If the results were similar, the candidate could comment that they were within 10 per cent of each other, or very close to each other.”

0625/61 · Question 1(a)(iii) · a difficulty with the measurement

“Many candidates scored partial credit with a relevant comment about the difficulty of measuring the diameter of a sphere with a ruler. Few identified parallax errors as the cause of the difficulty.”

That last one is worth noticing. “Parallax” dropped in at random earns nothing, but here it was the answer the examiners were looking for, because it named why that particular measurement was hard. The Paper 6 guide and the Paper 5 guide list the practical mistakes that keep coming back, with the number of the 17 series since 2021 that each one appears in.

A Level Physics 9702: what the June 2025 reports say

Paper 2 (AS structured questions)

9702/21 · Question 1(a) · defining acceleration

“Some candidates gave the definition of velocity. Other common errors were for candidates to mention ‘speed’ instead of velocity or to write ‘velocity over time’ which could imply a duration rather than a ratio.”

9702/21 · Question 2(a) · principle of moments

“Common errors included omitting ‘sum’, ‘about a point’ or ‘in equilibrium’. Some candidates confused momentum with moments; the phrase ‘in a closed system’ was often seen.”

9702/21 · Question 5(b)(ii) · Doppler effect

“Common mistakes included substituting 250 Hz as fs instead of fo, mixing up v with vs and using the wrong sign in the denominator of the equation.”

9702/21 · Key messages

“Candidates should be aware that rounding an intermediate answer in a numerical question can lead to an inaccurate final answer.”

Paper 4 (A Level structured questions)

9702/41 · Key messages

“Examples of words that are often confused by candidates are atom and molecule, nuclide and nucleus, and force and field.”

9702/41 · Key messages

“It is not uncommon to find candidates giving answers to questions that were not asked, but that have been asked in recent past papers. Candidates should be advised not to rely heavily on memorising previous mark schemes.”

9702/41 · Question 1(b)(i) · gravitational potential

“A common mistake was to equate the change in gravitational potential with the potential energy in the higher orbit (omitting the potential energy in the lower orbit).”

9702/41 · Question 2(b)(ii) · ‘show that’

“As always with a ‘show that’ question, marks are not awarded only for the answer, but for showing the working that leads to the answer.”

The second box is the one I'd pin above a desk. Past papers are the best practice there is, and the report says examiners keep seeing answers written for an earlier version of the question.

Paper 3 (Advanced Practical Skills)

9702/31 · Question 2(a) · recording readings

“A relatively common error was inconsistency of precision in the measurements given, and it was not uncommon to see data recorded such as 5.9, 6, 6.1.”

9702/31 · Question 1 · range of readings

“Candidates are expected to always use the whole range of data values available to them. There is no requirement for the independent variable to be evenly spaced.”

9702/32 · Question 2 · limitations

“Responses that refer to just ‘reaction time’ as a reason for inaccuracy in time values are unlikely to gain credit.”

9702/32 · Question 2 · limitations

“Some candidates made only very general suggestions of limitations, such as ‘inaccurate to use a metre rule to measure B’ or ‘air conditioning affects oscillations’, and these were not able to gain credit.”

The 9702/31 report says what does score: “Credit is awarded in this question for specifying the nature of the problem and giving a reason for it.” Name the quantity, say why it was hard to measure, then give a fix for that same problem. The limitations trainer drills this on ten real Paper 3 experiments.

Paper 5 (Planning, Analysis and Evaluation)

9702/51 · Question 1 · circuit diagram

“Common errors included voltmeters or oscilloscopes connected in series with the circuit, an a.c. supply not being specified, and an ohmmeter being connected in series or parallel with the circuit.”

9702/51 · Question 1 · additional detail

“Statements like ‘ensure all apparatus is working correctly’ or ‘check all connections are properly made’ will not gain credit as this is just normal good experimental procedure.”

9702/51 · Question 1 · safety

“When describing safety precautions, candidates should be encouraged to explain how the precaution proposed is relevant to the experiment. In this experiment, precautions when working with the hot coil or hot resistors were relevant and gained credit.”

9702/51 · Question 2(c)(i) · plotting

“A common error was in plotting (0.111, 1.46) as (0.101, 1.46). When plotting points, the diameter of each point should be less than half a small square.”

The linearisation trainer covers choosing the axes, and the uncertainties trainer covers the worst-acceptable-line working that every Paper 5 scheme from 2021 to 2026 has a mark for.

How to use this in revision

1. Find your own version of these mistakes. Do a past paper under timed conditions and mark it against the mark scheme. Then compare what you lost with the boxes above. If you'd rather have it marked by a former Cambridge examiner, I'll mark it for you.

2. Practise the habit, not the topic. If you lost a mark for “single colour”, the fix isn't more light revision. It's checking every definition you know against the syllabus wording.

3. Read one report yourself. Pick your next paper and read its section of the most recent report. It tells you what went wrong on that paper last time, in the examiners' own words.
